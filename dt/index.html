Digital Twin Work

Cities, mapped before the problem hits.

Two digital twins I built on real public data: one that shows which Utrecht neighbourhoods a heatwave hits hardest, and one that narrows down where to look first when a wastewater discharge is suspected.

01 · Veiligheidsregio Utrecht · Jun–Sep 2026

Heat Resilience Digital Twin

A heatwave does not hit a city evenly. It lands on people who are old, alone, or living under a roof with no shade. A safety region usually learns where after the fact. This twin puts it on a map beforehand: all 111 neighbourhoods in Gemeente Utrecht scored on 28 weighted indicators, then a drop to street level where every building carries the heat measured over it.

111CBS buurten scored
28weighted indicators
2,608real 3D buildings (BAG heights)
+2.5 °Chottest-class buildings vs the rest, checked against unseen Landsat data (40.9 vs 38.3 °C)

What it does

Six linked views from country to single building: Netherlands, province, city, resilience score, wijk ranking, and the 3D twin of two pilot neighbourhoods (Terwijde-Oost and Zambesidreef). Eleven candidate wijken across six municipalities are ranked and re-rankable by any single indicator.

  • CesiumJS
  • Python
  • geopandas
  • rasterio
  • QGIS
  • Landsat 8/9
  • KNMI

Why it can be trusted

Land surface temperature is labelled as surface temperature and never mixed with air temperature. Where CBS or RIVM suppress a value, the map says so instead of drawing a zero. Every indicator states which direction is good, read from the same flag the scoring uses. Handed over to the resilience team and the OKT-P programme.

02 · Space & Water Nexus Hackathon · 1–3 Oct 2026

Langeraar Water Quality Digital Twin

Challenge from Water Natuurlijk Rijnland: granular insight into wastewater discharges. Rijnland samples water quality at few places, weekly to monthly at best, so when something goes wrong between samples nobody can say where it started. Our twin of one polder (Noordeind- en Geerpolder) ranks the buildings and structures that are plausible places to start looking, and compares the two stations that have a real lab record.

4.66 km²pilot polder
2,238buildings scored (of 3,346)
492in the top screening tier
2 of 87registry stations have a lab record

What it does

3D BAG buildings, channels, lakes, weirs, pumps and discharge-type points on aerial imagery. Buildings are scored by distance to four discharge-type points and by use. A date slider steps through 81 lab dates and flags 15 readings that are unusual for their own station (Tukey fence).

  • CesiumJS
  • Vite
  • 3D BAG
  • PDOK
  • Rijnland Legger
  • QGIS

An honest screening tool

Scores are statistical signals from public data, not measurements, and they do not prove any building is a source. Grey means “not evaluated”, not “safe”. The flow layer is labelled illustrative. Confirming a source needs sampling on the ground.

04 · How I build them

Real data, stated limits, no sample numbers.

Data pipeline

Python (pandas, geopandas, rasterio, shapely) for ingestion and scoring. Sources include CBS, RIVM, KNMI, Klimaateffectatlas, USGS Landsat, PDOK BAG, 3D BAG, NWB roads, OpenStreetMap and Rijnland's Legger services.

Front end

CesiumJS for the 3D twins, single-file dashboards with no framework where possible, QGIS for spatial work, and Three.js for the scene at the top of this page, a stylised model, not project data.

The VRU source code is in a private repository because it is internship work for Veiligheidsregio Utrecht. Happy to walk through it on request.
